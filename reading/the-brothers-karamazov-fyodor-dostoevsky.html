The Brothers Karamazov

The first thing I’d say is The Brothers Karamazov is extremely readable, which was not what I was expecting. It was written in quite a jovial and fun tone and even without grasping any of the deeper points Dostoevsky gets into, it’s an enjoyable story. I’ve read a few philosophy books in the past, but not a fiction one like this. What helped me get more out of this was reading along with a book club (can now recommend Athenaeum Book Club).

Onto the book. Firstly, it’s a story about a murder and it’s set over only a couple of weeks (it somewhat ironically takes a lot longer than that to read). A majority of the book though is an extreme deep dive into the personalities of each of the characters, who have some pretty strong points of view on certain beliefs and morals. It’s frequently referred to as one of the great psychological novels and it really touches on a lot of topics from love to faith to passion to beauty and more. I wouldn’t profess to say I understood everything Dostoevsky was trying to say, but it did get me thinking.

The 3 brothers all have extremely strong personalities. Dmitry, the eldest, does everything at 100%. He’s a man of extremes and represents passion. Ivan is the highly intellectual, extremely rational brother who is frequently admired for some of the arguments he makes against god and faith. Alyosha (the youngest) is the spiritual one: the one who relies on a higher being to determine what he thinks and what he should do. He comes across as much more loving than the other two. The dad also plays a big role and he’s objectively a bad person. He has a lot of money but won’t give it to his kids. He actively chooses to play the role of a buffoon in public just because he likes that. He impregnated (I assume) a homeless and mentally ill girl to show off to his mates.

The whole family are representative of abundance. They didn’t have issues like a lot of the characters had. They had servants, they had money and they had each other. But they were still dysfunctional and unable to pull themselves together or work together to help each other. (I should note here that there are many, many other interesting characters but I won’t go into each one)

The most interesting thing to me in this novel is the difference pointed out between rationality and faith. I’ve always seen myself as guided much more by logic and I enjoy hashing things out rationally to get to a final answer. This is what we see in Ivan. The famous chapter is The Grand Inquisitor where he argues with Jesus Christ all the terrible things he’s brought into the world, and why would a god ever do that if they professed to be good. He argues it so well to his faith-loving brother Alyosha that Alyosha cannot respond at all. He simply kisses him on the forehead and goes on with his day. On the other hand is this Alyosha, who wants to commit his life to faith and never really says a bad word about anyone. He spends his early days at the monastery and is guided by an elder (a saint-like person) which informs every decision of him moving forward.

There’s no doubting Ivan’s intellect and some of the arguments he makes are truly brilliant. But in the end (I think this is a spoiler sorry), he loses his marbles, finds himself arguing with the devil in his mind and is clearly very disturbed by a number of the actions that took place in the book. There’s certainly no happy ending for Ivan.

On the other hand, Alyosha is left in the book bringing together a group of kids in celebrating one of their friends who had died. After a book filled with many unsavoury scenes, you’re left with this uplifting memory of Alyosha leading the group of boys through a difficult time. They are all in a circle and he brings joy to each of the boys in a very difficult time. They are taught valuable lessons - the fact that they’re all together, they all care for each other and whatever happens in the future, this is a time that they’re all loved.

A totally logical person could not provide such a message of hope. And as a dad of two children, is this not the most important message to pass on each and every day? I’m not saying I’m going to church tomorrow, but it does make me wonder how I can integrate more love into my life, and perhaps be a little bit less rational at times.
