# Quarterly Essay: Blind Spot

## Summary

A delightful new format of book I've found: in-depth essays on important issues involving Australia. As I'm about to move back there from the UAE, the $70 digital subscription seemed like a great way to get across some of the issues facing my favourite country (Australia).

This was a particularly interesting read on something that I'd never thought about in depth at all for Australia: geopolitics. Given I was in the UAE when the Iran/US war kicked off and saw first-hand what can happen when you're on the wrong side of geopolitics, this felt especially relevant.

And Michael Wesley explains the issues facing us clearly. We're too aligned with the US and not aligned enough with our geographically relevant countries. For a long time in Australia, there's been an automatic following of the US. But we've not treated countries like Indonesia, Malaysia and Papua New Guinea with any respect at all. Go and look on a globe and you'll see there are key waterways where a lot of goods and services transit (think fuel, pesticides, iron ore, coal, food and much more).

Where we could've made strong allies with geographically very relevant neighbours, we've left it open. And China has not made this mistake (this is not to say China is necessarily evil, which is the prevailing view in Australia). While we're certainly not enemies, we're not strong allies either. In the case of an escalating war, this means we don't know where we stand, when we could absolutely control our own destiny.
