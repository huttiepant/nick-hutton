Active Thinking

I’m currently building a few different tools to improve my thinking. I’m quite concerned about what the internet (and now AI) have done to my ability to think for myself, so I’m building projects that use AI to challenge me, not give me answers.

Mort

I believe there’s a huge difference between someone who reads and someone who reads deeply. Mort is a reading assistant to help you understand and remember more of what you read.

The premise is that you quickly log the book you’re reading and the pages you’ve just read. Then there are a few options if you want to go deeper:

  • It challenges you with a question specific to those pages.
  • It gives you a place to store any free-text notes you want.
  • It finds professor-level commentary on the internet related to that section and takes you to those articles, podcasts, videos etc.
  • It generates an AI overview of things you should be thinking about as you read that section (rather than a summary of the text).

All your notes are stored locally, and the more you read, the more Mort learns about you and your thinking. Over time, it will find connections, provide better recommendations and spot areas in your thinking that need attention.

Second Order

There is a lot of gold on the internet, but the thing is there’s far too much. This means people don’t take much of it seriously, so even great writing gets skimmed. Second Order challenges you to take more in from the articles you read.

When you read an article, you add it to Second Order and respond to it, either by voice or in writing. You can either summarise it and draw a conclusion, or argue with it (frameworks are provided to help you structure an argument). Then the coach will work with you to do two things:

  1. If there are any holes in your thinking, it will help you find them and get you to restate your case.
  2. Once you’ve understood the article, it will help you explore the second-order effects of your thinking. That is, the subsequent consequences of what you’ve just argued.

As with Mort, all the notes you capture are stored locally, so the more you read and think, the more the agent will learn about your thinking and be able to point you in useful directions.

Agora

Similar to Mort, Agora was built to help improve your reading skills, this time focusing specifically on philosophy books.

These books are hard to read, but with the right expert guidance, I believe anyone can get through them and learn a huge amount. So I teamed up with philosophy professors to annotate some of the classics, highlighting parts that a lot of people misunderstand and translating a lot of the language into more relatable messaging.

There are also end-of-chapter questions to promote discussion amongst people reading the book.

All these tools are designed to challenge my thinking, not give me answers.

The gym analogy is obvious, but I think it fits. If you go to the gym, do a few reps with 2kg dumbbells and leave, you’re not going to build much muscle. The same goes for your brain. If all you do is skim a few news sites, you’re not going to improve your thinking.

So I’m building these tools to help me find more desirable difficulties (ways to actually work on my thinking muscle).
